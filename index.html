How a credential travels

Five steps from the institution's desk to a verified printout. Nothing here needs technical knowledge.

Institution registers

It signs up with its registration number and picks a plan. Only registered institutions can issue.

Institution issues

It enters the owner's full details. Attest seals the record and creates a one-time code.

Owner confirms

The owner registers with the same details the institution holds, then enters the code to claim it.

Owner shares

The owner hands out a share code, chooses who sees details, and can request an approved hardcopy.

Anyone verifies

A subscribed verifier checks the code and prints a report: genuine or not.

Who uses Attest

Each role has its own sign-up, because each one needs to prove something different.

What you will see

Clear outcomes, on screen and on paper.

Genuine

The code is on the ledger and unaltered. You see the issuing institution and whether the owner confirmed it.

Not genuine

No record matches the code, or the institution has revoked the credential. The report says so plainly.

Under review

The integrity monitor held an unusual record for a human check. It is not trusted until a regulator clears it.

Regulator dashboards

Daily activity, risk scores, late-night clusters, institution comparisons and written findings, with an event log you can export.

Built to be trusted

The protections behind every check.

Tamper-proof records

Every credential gets a unique cryptographic fingerprint on a shared ledger. Change one letter and the fingerprint no longer matches.

No single point of failure

Records live on a distributed storage network rather than one server, so an outage or an attack on one machine cannot erase them.

Strict roles

Institutions issue. Owners control access. Verifiers check. Regulators audit. Anything outside a role is blocked and logged.

Integrity monitor

An AI-assisted monitor scores every event and flags forgery patterns such as late-night bursts or repeated lookups of fake codes.

Private by design

Only fingerprints are public. Owners decide who sees details and can erase their private file, in line with data-protection rules.

Fast and scalable

A check is a single lookup, with no waiting on a registrar. Plans scale from a small school to a national system.

Questions

Can a student register before their institution?

No. The institution must be registered on Attest first, because owners pick it from the registered list. This guarantees every credential can be verified.

How does an owner get their credential?

When the institution issues it, Attest creates a one-time code. The owner registers with the same details the institution holds and enters the code.

Who can verify?

Anyone with a verifier account: employers, schools deciding on admission, scholarship and licensing bodies, authorities or individuals. A plan is required, and each check can be printed.

Which credentials are covered?

Degrees, diplomas, certificates, transcripts and school leaving certificates from primary, junior and senior secondary schools, colleges, technical institutes and universities.

What if a credential is issued in error?

The issuing institution can revoke it. Every verifier then sees it as revoked.

Can owners get a paper copy?

Yes. With a Hardcopy Plus plan, an owner requests a copy. If the institution approves, the owner downloads it.
